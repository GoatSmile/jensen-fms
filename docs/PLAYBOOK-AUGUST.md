## Start here — two things, five minutes

### 1. Put yourself in the system

**Admin → People & roles → New person.** Your name and email, tick **Owner**
under Roles, then "Add person". That is the whole thing — one form.

Owner sees everything: every screen, every button. That is what you want
while you are exploring. Leave the rest of the fields as they are.

This changes nothing about how you log in — it is just the app knowing who
you are. Two things start working once you are on the list: **jobs can be
assigned to you by name** (open any work order and you will see an assignee),
and you become someone the app can notify when something needs attention.
Right now I am the only name in there, which is not much use to anyone.

### 2. Choose your language

**Admin → Settings → General.** Two settings:

- **App language** — the office side: orders, invoices, customers, admin.
- **Workshop language** — the shop-floor screens the mechanics use.

Set both to whatever you prefer. They are independent on purpose: you might
want Danish on the floor and English in the office, or the same for both.

It takes effect immediately, and you can change it as often as you like.
I have checked every screen in Danish and it is complete — nothing falls
back to English. Your call entirely.

## Then: the part that actually matters

**Use the app like it is already your system, and write down everything you
notice.**

Not bug reports — just reactions. The things worth capturing:

- **"This is not how we actually do it."** The most valuable thing you can
  tell me. I built from what I understood; you know the real job.
- **"I expected this to be here and it wasn't."**
- **"I don't know what this word means"** or "we call this something else."
- **"This takes too many clicks."**
- **"This screen is confusing"** — even if you can't say why yet.
- **"Can it also do…?"**

A note on your phone or a sheet of paper is fine. Page name plus one line is
plenty — I do not need detail, I need your reactions. Every item becomes a
decision when we meet.

**Nothing you click can break anything.** Create test parts, fake customers,
practice orders, whatever helps. The database is backed up every night. Make
a mess — I would rather you explored freely and we tidied up after than that
you were careful and learned nothing.

Two exceptions at the very bottom of this page.

## A suggested hour, if you want a route

Not required — but this order touches most of the app and mirrors a real
week.

1. **Dashboard.** What needs you today. Money at the top, then your
   pipelines. If a card is not there, nothing needs you.
2. **A bike.** Bikes → pick one → read its page. History, parts, photos,
   its QR code, who owns it.
3. **The workshop floor, on your phone.** Open **Work → Workshop floor** on
   your actual phone, not the computer. That is how your mechanics will meet
   this app, and it is built for gloves and bad light. Try **Scan** too.
4. **A repair, start to finish.** Work → Tickets → New ticket against a real
   bike. Then "Start work order", add a couple of parts, mark it done. Watch
   the ticket close itself and the job appear as ready to invoice.
5. **A build.** Orders → Manufacturing → pick an order → open a bike →
   Build. This is where a bike gets assembled: confirm the frame number,
   pull in the recipe, finish the build. Stock comes off automatically.
6. **Print something.** A purchase order, a picking list, a sheet of QR
   stickers. These are the paper your shop actually runs on — they should
   look right coming off the printer.
7. **Your customers on a map.** Customers → Map. Where your bikes are, whose
   agreements are running out.

## Where your expert eye is worth most

If you have appetite beyond exploring, these are the places where only you
can tell whether the app is right. All four are *checking*, not data entry.

- **The bike recipes.** Bikes → Bike templates → open one. Is that genuinely
  what goes into that bike, in those quantities? These lists drive costing,
  purchasing and picking, so an error here spreads everywhere.
- **Your painter's prices.** Admin → Services. Your real price lists,
  including the quantity breaks. Do the numbers match what Metacoat
  actually charges?
- **Part names and categories.** Parts → All parts. Anything named
  confusingly, filed in the wrong category, or duplicated?
- **Stock figures will look wrong, and that is expected.** Opening stock was
  never entered, so some parts show odd or negative numbers — you will see
  one part sitting at minus 207. That is not a fault; the app only knows
  what it has watched come in and go out. We fix it with one physical count
  on the morning we go live, not by typing.

## What is new since you last used it

In plain terms:

- **The whole app speaks Danish**, and now so does every last screen.
- **It looks like Jensen** rather than a generic admin tool. Six colours
  with fixed meanings — blue for navigation and main actions, sand/gold for
  money and "worth a look", green for ready and on schedule, red only for
  something genuinely wrong, rust for buying and suppliers, purple for
  settings and agreements. Red is deliberately rare, so if you see it, it
  means you.
- **The left menu is seven groups instead of a long list**, grouped the way
  you talk about the job. Open a group and it stays how you left it.
- **All the drop-down lists live on one page**: Admin → Lists. Categories,
  colours, coatings, customer segments, bike families, HS codes, stock
  locations, paint part types — tabs down the side. Old bookmarks still
  work; they just bring you here.
- **Phone calls become tickets.** A call to the test number is transcribed
  and turned into a draft ticket waiting in **Inbox**. A human always
  reviews before anything happens.
- **Paint became a general "services" model** with your price lists and
  quantity breaks, so washing or priming can join later without rebuilding.
- **People & roles** — each role can eventually get its own login showing
  only their part of the app. Built, not switched on. We do that with the
  team when I am back.
- **The dashboard got the money band and trend charts.**

Nothing moved address. Every bookmark you have still lands where it did.

I also spent a full day testing the app end to end — every screen, and every
job from creating a bike through to invoicing — and fixed what it turned up.
There is nothing outstanding that I know of. Which is exactly why your fresh
eyes are worth more than more of mine.

## Two things not to press

Everything else is fair game.

1. **Do not press "Issue" on an invoice.** Drafts are completely safe —
   make as many as you like. But issuing one permanently takes an invoice
   number out of the official sequence, and that sequence has to be clean
   and gapless for your accountant. Look at the draft, look at the print
   preview, then leave it.
2. **Leave the switches alone** — supplier email is in test mode, the phone
   pipeline is in review-only mode, and e-conomic is still pointed at a
   practice account. All three are one click from being real, and all three
   should be flipped with both of us in the room. We do that mid-August.
